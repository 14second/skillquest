How to spot a weak claim

  • Ask: can it be proven true or false? If yes, it is a fact — or a claim waiting for evidence. If it is a feeling or a judgment (“best”, “boring”, “should”), it is an opinion.
  • Watch for weasel words: “everyone knows”, “studies show”, “most people”, “scientists agree”, “proven”. If no study, person, or number is named, the claim is unsupported.
  • “Always” and “never” are warning signs. One huge word can turn a small truth into a false claim.
  • One story is not proof. “My cousin tried it and it worked” is an anecdote. A fair test compares two groups that are alike except for the one thing being tested.
  • Ask who benefits if you believe it. Ads and sales pages want you to believe their claim — that is a reason to check, not a reason to trust.
  • Strong evidence is specific and checkable: a measurement, a fair comparison, or a real study you can look up. Weak evidence is a feeling, one person's story, or something unrelated.
